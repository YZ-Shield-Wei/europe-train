European train travel: frequently asked questions

Ten practical answers to check before you buy and before you board. Your actual ticket conditions and the operator’s instructions take precedence.

Sources checked: 30 October 2026

How do I book European train tickets?

Compare the railway’s own website or app with a ticket retailer. Check the exact stations, date, passengers, seat inclusion, fees and refund conditions before paying. Keep the booking confirmation and note who sold the ticket; that seller is usually your first contact for a voluntary change.

How far in advance should I book?

There is no Europe-wide cheapest booking window. Sales opening dates vary by operator, route and engineering timetable. Once your travel date is available, compare the actual price and conditions. An unavailable departure may not yet be on sale; do not assume it is sold out.

What is a Eurail or Interrail Pass?

Eurail joined the Interrail brand in September 2026. Current options include Global and One Country passes; do not rely on old Select Pass descriptions. Compare the pass price plus required reservations and supplements with individual tickets for your itinerary. A pass does not guarantee a seat.

Do I need a seat reservation?

Check the exact train and date. Reservations may be mandatory, optional or unavailable; not every high-speed train follows the same rule. Your ticket may already include a seat. With a pass, check the separate reservation requirement and the specific product inclusions before buying another reservation.

Can I change or refund my ticket?

Read the conditions of the fare you actually buy: deadline, fee, fare difference, refund method and sales channel. “Flexible” is not a universal promise of a free cash refund. Voluntary changes and disruption-related rights are different processes; contact the seller or operator as appropriate.

How do children’s tickets work?

Age limits, free travel, accompaniment and seat requirements differ by operator and product. Enter every child with the correct age when searching, even if travel might be free. Check whether a separate ticket, reservation or sleeping berth is required; there is no single European child discount.

What are the luggage rules?

Check the operator’s limits for your route and class, including number, dimensions and any restrictions on bikes, sports equipment or oversized items. Do not apply one railway’s allowance to all European trains. Plan how to lift and store your bags without blocking aisles or doors.

Can I claim compensation for a delay?

For covered EU rail journeys, compensation is generally 25% for 60–119 minutes of arrival delay and 50% for 120 minutes or more, subject to conditions and exceptions. Refund and rerouting rights are separate. Keep evidence and use the operator’s claim procedure; not every service or incident qualifies.

How do I collect and use my ticket?

Follow the delivery and validation instructions in your confirmation. Depending on the product, use an app, printable ticket or station collection. A payment receipt alone may not be a valid ticket. Download the required document before departure and check whether activation, validation or identification is required.

Which payment methods can I use?

Use the methods shown by your chosen seller at checkout; availability can depend on country, currency and channel. Review the final total and any card or currency-conversion costs. If payment fails, check for an existing booking or pending charge before trying again to avoid duplicates.
