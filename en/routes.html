Choose stations and connections

Check the complete station name, not just the city: Paris has several mainline stations, and an airport stop is not the city centre. Allow time for station changes, luggage and assistance. For a connection, ask whether the itinerary is sold as a through-ticket or as separate contracts.

Official sources: SNCF Connect

International and domestic journeys

Use the operator in each row to search the route on your intended day. Compare direct and connecting trains, arrival station, total door-to-door time and fare conditions. International travel can involve different boarding formalities and ticket contracts. The list does not promise a direct train, a fixed duration or a minimum price.

Official sources: Eurostar · DB

Scenic routes and night trains

Glacier Express, Bernina Express and GoldenPass are different products with their own booking rules. Compare the panoramic service with ordinary trains on the relevant corridor, and check pass validity and supplements separately. For Semmering or a night-train itinerary, use the current ÖBB timetable; do not assume a historic Thello service is bookable.

Official sources: Glacier Express · Rhätische Bahn · GoldenPass Express · ÖBB Nightjet

Compare the full cost

Compare the same date, class, passenger ages and baggage allowance. Add booking charges, seat reservations, payment currency costs and transfers. A retailer may offer useful language or payment support, but neither an operator nor an aggregator is always cheapest. Keep a screenshot of the final conditions before paying.

Boarding and accessibility

Follow the arrival time and gate deadline on your ticket. Eurostar journeys to or from London involve pre-departure border and luggage checks; other Eurostar routes have different arrangements. Check travel documents with the relevant authorities. Arrange required assistance with the operator and verify the boarding point and station accessibility.

Official sources: Eurostar
