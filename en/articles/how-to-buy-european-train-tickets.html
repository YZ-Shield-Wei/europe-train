THE PRACTICAL GUIDE

How to buy European train tickets

Start with the journey, not the discount. A useful ticket is one you can actually use, with enough time to connect and conditions that fit your plans.

The short answer

Check the operating railway and a reputable retailer for the same journey. Compare the final price and the fare rules, not just the first price shown. Buy before boarding unless the operator explicitly permits another arrangement.

1. Confirm the stations and the connection

Write down your travel date, the full station names, passenger ages and luggage or accessibility needs. Cities can have several stations. A change of station can mean crossing the city, not changing platforms.

For a connection, check whether the booking is a through-ticket or separate tickets, and what protection the seller says applies if the first train is late. Our advice: allow more recovery time when travelling with children, heavy luggage, an airport connection or the last train of the day. There is no universal safe 15-minute connection.

London Eurostar journeys have pre-departure border and luggage checks. Follow the arrival time on your ticket and the current Eurostar: when to arrive at the station; arriving just before departure is not enough.

2. Compare the same journey and the final total

Begin with the railway operating your train, such as DB, SNCF Connect, Trenitalia or Eurostar. Omio, Trainline and Rail Europe are independent retail channels, not the official apps of every railway. A retailer may offer convenient language, payment and itinerary options; it is not automatically cheaper or more expensive.

  • Match the date, departure, stations, class, passenger ages and discount-card eligibility.
  • Check the total after any booking fee, optional seat, luggage or other selected extras. Check the payment currency too.
  • Read change and cancellation deadlines, fees, fare differences and refund method. “Flexible” does not mean every train or an unconditional cash refund.
  • If the train is not on sale, check the operator’s booking window and engineering notices. Missing results do not prove that the train is sold out.

Use the actual quote for your date. We do not publish a universal cheapest booking window, fixed savings percentage or guaranteed lowest-price seller.

3. Price a pass against your actual itinerary

Compare the total of your individual tickets with the pass price plus required reservations, supplements and journeys outside the pass coverage. Include the value of flexibility, not just the number of countries visited.

Since 9 September 2026, Eurail has been joining the Interrail brand for European and non-European residents. Existing passes are not invalidated by the name change. Check the current product and residence conditions rather than choosing solely from an old “Eurail versus Interrail” chart. Interrail: Eurail brand transition.

A pass does not automatically secure a seat. Before buying it, check whether the trains essential to your itinerary can be reserved. See our seat-reservation guide.

4. Check these details before paying

  1. Confirm every traveller, date, station and class. If you claim a discount, make sure you have the required card or document.
  2. Check whether the ticket includes a seat, requires a separate reservation, or has open seating.
  3. Find out how you receive the usable ticket: app, PDF, collection or another specified method. A payment receipt is not always the travel document.
  4. Save the seller’s booking reference, fare conditions and customer-service route. Know who will handle a voluntary change.
  5. Only confirm once the complete price and the restrictions suit your plans.

5. Prepare the ticket before departure

Open the actual ticket in the required format before reaching the platform. Check any validation or activation instructions, and bring the identity or discount documents required for your fare. Some digital products use a live in-app display; a screenshot is not a universal substitute.

Do not assume you can buy from the conductor. DB has specific limits on post-departure digital purchases, while regional rules vary. Buying in advance avoids relying on an exception. DB: purchasing tickets after boarding.

Check the operator’s disruption information, your departure station and boarding instructions again near travel time. Our website provides planning guidance, not live confirmation of train operation.
