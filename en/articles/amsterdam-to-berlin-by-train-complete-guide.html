Amsterdam to Berlin by Train: The Complete 2026 Guide

Travel from Amsterdam to Berlin by train with ease. The 660 km route connects two of Europe's most vibrant capitals through scenic Dutch and German countryside, with direct ICE International and InterCity services making the journey comfortable and convenient.

Train Options

ICE International (Deutsche Bahn / NS)

The fastest and most comfortable option. ICE International trains run directly from Amsterdam Centraal to Berlin Hauptbahnhof with a journey time of approximately 6 hours and 20 minutes. These modern high-speed trains offer:

  • Free WiFi throughout the journey
  • Power outlets at every seat
  • Onboard restaurant car serving hot meals and drinks
  • First and Second Class options
  • Quiet zones for peaceful travel

Key stops include: Utrecht, Arnhem, Oberhausen, Duisburg, Düsseldorf, Cologne, Montabaur, Frankfurt Airport, Frankfurt Main, Erfurt, Weimar, Naumburg, Leipzig, Lutherstadt Wittenberg, and Berlin.

InterCity (IC)

A more economical alternative with a longer journey time of around 7.5 to 8.5 hours. IC trains require one transfer, typically in Hannover or Osnabrück. While slower, they offer comfortable seating and are ideal for budget-conscious travelers.

Schedule & Frequency

Direct ICE International trains operate up to 6 times daily:

  • First departure: Amsterdam Centraal at 06:38, arriving Berlin Hbf at 12:58
  • Last departure: Amsterdam Centraal at 18:38, arriving Berlin Hbf at 00:58

InterCity services run more frequently but require connections. It's recommended to book in advance, especially for Friday afternoons and Sunday evenings when demand is highest.

Where to Buy

Recommended: Book directly through Deutsche Bahn or NS International for the best prices. Both websites offer English language options and accept international credit cards.

Alternative booking platforms include Trainline, Omio, and Rail Europe, though these may charge small booking fees.

Station Information

Amsterdam Centraal

The main railway hub of the Netherlands, located in the city center. The station offers excellent connections to Amsterdam's tram and metro network. Arrive at least 15 minutes before departure for international trains.

Berlin Hauptbahnhof

Europe's largest crossing station, opened in 2006. Located in the heart of Berlin with easy access to the U-Bahn, S-Bahn, and bus networks. The station features numerous shops, restaurants, and luggage storage facilities.

Travel Tips

  • Book early: Super Sparpreis tickets are limited and sell out quickly, especially during peak travel seasons
  • Consider a stopover: Cologne is an excellent city to break your journey, with the magnificent cathedral right next to the station
  • Bring snacks: While the onboard restaurant is good, bringing your own food can save money
  • Download entertainment: The Dutch and German countryside is beautiful, but 6+ hours is a long journey
  • Check passport requirements: While both countries are in the Schengen Area, carry ID for border checks

Scenic Highlights

The route offers diverse landscapes:

  • Dutch polders: Flat, green landscapes with iconic windmills near Utrecht
  • Rhine-Ruhr region: Industrial heritage and modern cities around Düsseldorf and Cologne
  • Rhine Valley: Brief glimpse of the famous river valley (on some routes)
  • Thuringian Forest: Rolling hills and forests between Frankfurt and Leipzig
  • Saxon countryside: Historic towns and landscapes approaching Berlin

FAQ

Is there a direct train from Amsterdam to Berlin?

Yes, ICE International offers direct services up to 6 times daily with a journey time of approximately 6 hours and 20 minutes.

How much does the train from Amsterdam to Berlin cost?

Prices start from €37.90 for advance-purchase Super Sparpreis tickets. Standard fares range from €47.90 to €120+ depending on flexibility.

Do I need to reserve a seat?

Seat reservations are optional but recommended, especially on Friday afternoons and weekends. They cost approximately €4.50 when booked with your ticket.

Can I use a Eurail Pass on this route?

Yes, both Eurail and Interrail passes are valid on this route. However, seat reservations are mandatory on ICE International trains and cost approximately €5.90.

Is there WiFi on the train?

Yes, ICE International trains offer free WiFi throughout the journey in both First and Second Class.
