Practical route guide

London to Edinburgh by LNER

Plan the journey around your travel date, exact stations and ticket conditions. This is a booking guide, not a live timetable or a fare quotation.

1. Route, stations and journey time

Search London King’s Cross to Edinburgh Waverley for your actual date. King’s Cross is not the same station as nearby St Pancras, and Edinburgh Waverley is not Haymarket. LNER offers direct trains on this route, but stops, duration and engineering diversions vary. Use the selected departure’s full timetable rather than treating the old guide’s journey time as a promise. Check your return separately before planning a day trip.

2. Tickets, flexibility and discounts

Compare LNER’s current ticket options rather than the old fixed-price table. Advance is tied to a specific departure; a flexible ticket has its own route, operator and time restrictions. If 70min Flex is offered, read its permitted time window and conditions instead of treating it as an unrestricted ticket. Price outward and return journeys independently, including any Railcard discount for which each passenger qualifies. Carry the required Railcard and check minimum-fare and time restrictions. No fixed booking lead time guarantees the lowest fare.

3. Seats, Azuma and luggage

Check whether the booking includes a seat and select a suitable place if travelling together matters. LNER’s reservation tool can add a reservation to an eligible existing ticket; it does not replace the travel ticket needed at the barriers. For a rail pass, first check UK and LNER coverage, then arrange a seat separately if needed. Azuma offers onboard Wi-Fi and power, but keep tickets and essential work available offline; equipment and connectivity can fail. Standard and First Class catering differ, so check the service offered on your departure rather than expecting a hot meal with every fare.

4. Views and onward journeys

Before boarding, check LNER’s current baggage dimensions and allowances and reserve a bicycle space when required. Arrange assistance in advance if you need it, and allow time to reach the platform with luggage. The East Coast route offers changing city, countryside and coastal views; daylight, weather, diversions and the exact service affect what you see. York, Newcastle and Berwick-upon-Tweed are possible itinerary interests, not guaranteed stops on every train. To break the journey, check your ticket permits a stopover or buy an appropriate separate itinerary.

5. Prepare for departure

Keep the ticket and any separate reservation available offline, with the travel documents and discount evidence required for your booking. Check the operator’s luggage, bicycle, pet and accessibility rules in advance; do not assume every train has the same facilities. Arrange assistance or a bicycle space before travel when required. Recheck service information and the departure board rather than relying on an old screenshot.

6. If plans change

For a voluntary change, read the deadline, fee, price difference and refund method in your fare conditions. For a cancellation or a missed connection caused by disruption, follow the operator’s instructions and keep evidence. Separate tickets may not offer the same connection protection as a through ticket. Allow a realistic margin for changing platforms or stations, and consider an overnight stop rather than building a trip around the last possible connection.
