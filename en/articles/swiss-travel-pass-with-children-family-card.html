Swiss Travel Pass with children: a practical Swiss Family Card guide

Start with each child’s age and accompanying parent, then price the adults’ itinerary. A free child card does not make every family trip—or every mountain excursion—free.

Who qualifies for the Swiss Family Card?

The complimentary Swiss Family Card is for children aged at least 6 and under 16 travelling with at least one parent holding a valid Swiss Travel Pass, Swiss Travel Pass Flex or Swiss Half Fare Card. With the Half Fare Card, the parent also needs the appropriate journey ticket. These visitor products require permanent residence outside Switzerland and Liechtenstein.

Children under 6 travel free; they do not need this 6–15 child card. A child travelling only with a grandparent, friend or other adult does not qualify simply because that adult has a pass. Check the appropriate child ticket instead.

Six checks before you choose the parent’s pass

Age: record each child’s birth date and age on the travel dates. If a 6th or 16th birthday falls during the trip, ask SBB how the transition affects the relevant journeys before ordering.

Relationship: identify which parent will accompany each child on each day. Parent’s ticket: check product, class, validity dates and any Flex travel-day requirement. Child’s card: include each eligible child in the order and verify that their own card is issued.

Route: check every mountain segment and any route outside the validity area. Seats: check whether reservations are required for adults and children, including a child who needs a separate seat.

Mountains and reserved seats need separate checks

Do not treat the Swiss Family Card as an all-attractions ticket. Check the official validity area and each mountain operator’s child-card acceptance, any additional ticket and the exact route. A parent’s discount does not by itself tell you the child’s entitlement.

For a panoramic train, separate the journey fare from its seat reservation and supplement. SBB expressly excludes reservations and/or surcharges from the free panoramic-train benefit. Check availability for the whole family before fixing pass dates; a free journey does not reserve adjacent seats.

Three example families, three different decisions

Illustrative family A: two parents and children aged 5 and 9 travel between several cities. The 5-year-old does not need the Swiss Family Card; add the 9-year-old when purchasing a qualifying parent product. Compare the adults’ total journey costs, not four adult passes.

Illustrative family B: one parent travels with children aged 12 and 16. Check the 12-year-old’s family card, but budget separately for the 16-year-old, who is outside this age band.

Illustrative family C: a grandparent travels alone with a 10-year-old. Do not budget for free travel under a parent-only rule. Ask SBB about a suitable child ticket and calculate the combined cost. These examples are planning scenarios, not fare quotations.

Is the parent’s Swiss Travel Pass worth buying?

A pass can suit a family moving between bases on several covered travel days and valuing flexibility. Make a day-by-day list of the parents’ covered journeys, extra mountain costs and everyone’s reservations. Then compare that total with individual tickets or the Swiss Half Fare Card plus tickets.

The Swiss Family Card is also available with the Swiss Half Fare Card, so its availability alone is not a reason to buy the more extensive pass. If the family mostly stays in one resort, price that local plan first. Keep a weather alternative rather than buying around an ambitious mountain checklist.

What to check before paying

In SBB’s purchase process, the Swiss Family Card must be obtained together with the qualifying parent product, not bought separately or added later. Before paying a reseller, confirm how each child is included and how the card is delivered; do not assume the adult voucher alone covers this step.

Check names, dates of birth, parent pass dates and class, refund rules and the delivered documents. Carry the child’s original valid passport or official ID: SBB does not accept a photo or copy for this card’s inspection. Save tickets and reservations for access during the journey.
