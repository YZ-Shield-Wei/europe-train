Practical route guide

Rome to Florence by train: stations, Frecciarossa and Italo tickets

Compare Rome–Florence trains by station, operator and ticket flexibility, not just the first price shown. This guide helps you choose and prepare; use the official booking results for your date, timetable and available seats.

Roma → Firenze

1. Choose the station you actually need

Start by checking Roma Termini or Roma Tiburtina and Firenze Santa Maria Novella, often shortened to Firenze S. M. Novella or SMN. Italo lists both Rome stations and Firenze S.M.Novella on its route page. Read the exact stations in the result before paying; a city-wide search can include a station different from the one you intended.

Termini and Tiburtina are not interchangeable boarding points. Likewise, Firenze Campo di Marte and Firenze Rifredi are not Santa Maria Novella. Include the journey between your accommodation and the chosen stations when comparing options. For the return, repeat this check rather than assuming the same stations will appear automatically.

2. Compare Frecciarossa and Italo as separate operators

Trenitalia offers Frecciarossa services on the Rome–Florence route, while Italo sells its own high-speed services. Compare both official searches for your actual date, direction and party. A ticket for one operator is not a general ticket for any train between the cities; confirm the operator and train number on your booking.

Compare the whole result: stations, departure and arrival, any changes, class and the final total. Comfort class and fare flexibility are different choices. A higher-class seat does not by itself mean that your ticket can be changed or refunded without restrictions.

3. Buy the flexibility your plans require

Trenitalia’s Base, Economy and Super Economy fares have different change and refund rules. Base is more flexible, while Economy and Super Economy impose tighter limits. Optional refund products and special offers can alter the conditions. Read the exact ticket terms rather than relying on a general label such as “discounted ticket”.

Italo’s Flex, Economy and Low Cost fares also differ. A permitted change can involve a fee, a fare difference, or both; deadlines depend on the offer. Check these before buying, particularly if you will arrive in Rome by air. Do not assume that a delayed flight entitles you to take a later train on the same ticket.

Keep the original seller’s booking reference and instructions for changes. If you book a feeder journey separately, allow a sensible margin and check the protection applying to those bookings. Voluntary cancellation and assistance during a rail disruption are different questions.

4. Use the correct ticket and validation process

For a booked high-speed journey, check the train, carriage and seat details and keep the ticket or booking code ready for inspection. Do not board an earlier or later train without following the change rules. Use the station departure board to find the current platform and confirm the train number, not only the final destination.

If your itinerary includes a Trenitalia regional train, identify the ticket type. Digital Regional Tickets validate automatically at the selected train’s scheduled departure; the former manual check-in step is no longer required. Single paper regional tickets must be validated before departure. Electronic Regional Tickets are another category with their own rules. Do not apply one ticket type’s validity window to another.

5. Check luggage, seats and travellers before payment

Review the seats allocated to your party and enter children’s ages accurately. Do not assume that separate bookings will place everyone together or that a child travelling free necessarily has a seat. If assistance is needed, arrange it through the operator’s accessibility service rather than leaving the request until boarding.

Luggage conditions depend on the operator and travel class. Italo’s published Smart-class limit for a bag is 75 × 53 × 30 cm; check the appropriate rules before choosing a fare if your suitcase is larger. Store bags in the designated spaces and keep doors and aisles clear. Check separate rules for bicycles, pets or unusual items.

6. Compare a normal ticket with the right pass rules

For a covered Frecciarossa journey with an Interrail Pass, a seat reservation is required in addition to the pass. Check coverage, class, travel-day validity and the reservation charge before boarding. A pass alone is not the complete booking for a train requiring a reservation.

Trenitalia Pass is a different product for eligible foreign citizens living outside Italy. Its covered trains require reservations, which are included in the pass; it does not cover Italo. Each booked train counts as a journey, so a direct Rome–Florence trip uses one journey. A separate direct Florence–Venice trip adds another, not a third merely because you visit three cities.

Compare the pass with the cost and flexibility of your whole itinerary, not this leg alone. Before payment, confirm stations, travellers, operator, class, seats and change conditions. Before travel, recheck the service and save the required documents. This page does not promise that a pass or a particular operator will always be cheaper.
