PRACTICAL RAIL GUIDE

Swiss Travel Pass or Swiss Half Fare Card? Build your own comparison

Neither product is automatically the best choice. Compare the complete itinerary using eligible fares and the official validity maps.

Start with a journey list

List every intended rail, bus, boat and mountain journey with dates, passengers and class. Separate city travel from expensive excursions and mark days when you might not travel. Check eligibility and family conditions for each product; do not assume a child, resident or companion is covered simply because the adult holds a pass.

Calculate both totals

For Swiss Travel Pass, start with the pass price and add reservations, supplements and journeys not fully covered. For Swiss Half Fare Card, add the card price to the actually applicable reduced tickets for every planned journey. Use the official area-of-validity maps: neither a name nor a headline discount proves the exact mountain segment is included.

Test the uncertain parts

Calculate a second version in which weather cancels an expensive excursion or you take a rest day. Compare convenience and flexibility as well as money. Confirm refund conditions before buying either product, and do not count a hypothetical discount twice. Keep the calculation dated because fares and product conditions may change.

What to compare

Compare the same travel date, stations, passengers and accommodation. The cheapest result can become poor value once baggage, reservations, local transfers or restrictive changes are included. Save the complete offer and its conditions, not just the headline price.

Before you pay

Check the departure and arrival station names carefully. A city can have several stations, and a transfer across town needs more time than a change on adjacent platforms. There is no safe universal connection time: allow for luggage, children, access needs and the consequences of missing the next departure.

Connections and travel day

Keep your ticket, any separate reservation and required identity documents accessible offline. Recheck the operator’s travel information shortly before departure. If a disruption occurs, follow instructions for your actual booking and keep notifications and receipts; voluntary changes and disruption arrangements are different processes.

A practical final check

Before purchasing, write down the final cost, what is included, the change or cancellation deadline, and where help will come from. If an important condition is unclear, ask the seller before committing. A timetable entry is not a guarantee that a seat, berth or particular onboard facility is available.
