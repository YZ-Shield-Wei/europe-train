THE PRACTICAL GUIDE

Do you need a seat reservation?

A valid ticket and a reserved seat are different things. The right answer depends on the specific train and the product you bought, not simply whether it is “high-speed”.

Check this first

Does your booking already show a coach and seat or sleeping accommodation? If it does, check the confirmation before paying for another reservation. If not, find the reservation rule for your exact train and date.

1. Start with the ticket you actually hold

A point-to-point fare may already include a seat assignment; another fare may cover travel without assigning a seat. A reservation-only document generally needs the appropriate travel ticket or pass alongside it. Keep both documents when they are separate.

Do not use a single Europe-wide fee table as a quote. Availability, service, class, booking channel and accommodation can change the cost. Compare the amount and conditions shown at checkout.

2. Identify which of these three cases applies

  • Mandatory reservation: obtain the required reservation before travelling. A pass or flexible ticket alone does not remove that requirement.
  • Optional reservation: a valid travel ticket can allow boarding without a seat assignment, but it does not guarantee somewhere to sit.
  • No reservations offered: there is no seat to pre-book through a different seller; follow the train’s open-seating rules.

Interrail: optional reservations explains these distinctions for pass holders. Many night and high-speed services require booking, but “all high-speed trains require a reservation” is incorrect.

ICE is a good reason to check the actual service. DB sells optional reservations, while some international routes or seasonal periods require them. See DB: long-distance seat reservations and NS International: ICE reservations for the relevant route and travel date.

3. A rail pass is not a seat booking

Check reservations for the trains that matter most before committing to a pass-based itinerary. The reservation step and adding a journey to your mobile pass are separate: saving a journey in a planner does not book a seat.

A standard pass commonly needs separately paid reservations where required. Some Plus Pass products include reservation credits subject to their own conditions and availability; these are not unlimited seats on any train. Check Interrail Plus Pass: purchase conditions rather than assuming every pass has the same inclusions.

Before boarding with a mobile pass, follow the activation and journey-adding steps and be able to display its ticket. See Interrail: using a mobile Pass. Keep any separate reservation accessible too.

4. If you cannot reserve the train

  1. Confirm you searched with the correct date, route, passenger details and pass-holder option where applicable.
  2. Check whether reservations have opened and whether that booking channel supports the service. Ask the operator or pass reservation service if the result is unclear.
  3. Try another departure, route or travel day. If considering a train without mandatory reservations, check total travel time and the last onward connection.
  4. Compare an ordinary ticket only after checking its price and conditions. Do not buy it on the assumption that a pass-related refund is guaranteed.

A displayed timetable is not proof of seats being available. If a required reservation cannot be obtained, do not board on the strength of a pass alone.

5. Before you board

  • Match the reservation’s date, train, stations and class to your travel ticket or pass.
  • Check all travellers, including children who need their own reservation or accommodation.
  • For overnight travel, confirm seat, couchette or sleeper and the precise accommodation booked.
  • For a change of train, check both the travel entitlement and the reservation. Changing one does not necessarily change the other.
