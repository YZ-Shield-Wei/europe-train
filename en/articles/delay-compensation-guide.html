Train delays: what to do and how to make a claim

Start by protecting the rest of your journey, then keep the evidence for a claim. A voluntary ticket refund, alternative transport and delay compensation are different processes.

Check which rules apply

EU rail passenger-rights rules have a defined scope and some service exemptions. They are not a single policy for every train in Europe. Check the carrier, countries, ticket contract and whether connecting tickets form a through-ticket. Separate purchases can have different protection, even when booked close together.

Choose your next step

For covered journeys where arrival is expected to be at least 60 minutes late, refund or continuation/rerouting options may apply. Ask the operator what it authorises before arranging expensive alternatives. Assistance may also be due. Do not assume a taxi, flight or hotel chosen independently will automatically be reimbursed.

Understand compensation

For eligible covered journeys, the EU baseline is 25% of the ticket price for arrival delay of 60–119 minutes and 50% for 120 minutes or more. Exceptions apply, including certain extraordinary circumstances and cases where a refund was chosen. An exception to compensation does not automatically remove rerouting, refund or assistance rights.

Keep useful evidence

Save the booking reference, ticket, original itinerary, disruption notice, actual arrival time and necessary receipts. Record what the railway told you and how you contacted it. Do not publish booking barcodes, identity documents or passenger details when asking for help on social media.

Submit the right request

Use the carrier's official passenger-rights form or the appropriate booking channel. Specify whether you want ticket reimbursement, eligible extra costs or delay compensation. Check its time limit and required documents. DB provides its own claim routes; other operators may use different forms and eligibility rules.

If the answer is unclear

Ask for the reason in writing and retain the case number. The EU passenger-rights page explains complaint and escalation routes, including the relevant national authority. The links below are the reference for a specific case; this checklist does not promise compensation or replace case-specific advice.
