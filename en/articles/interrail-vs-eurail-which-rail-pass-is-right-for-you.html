Eurail and Interrail: what changed and how to choose a pass

Older advice that simply assigns Eurail to non-European residents and Interrail to European residents is no longer a reliable way to choose a product.

The brand change

The official announcement says Eurail became part of the Interrail brand from 9 September 2026. Both European and non-European residents now see Interrail products. Existing Eurail passes and bookings remain valid under the transition; a change of name does not itself require buying a replacement pass.

Choose coverage and travel days

Start with the countries and train journeys in your itinerary. Compare Global and One Country products, the applicable pass area, validity period, number of travel days and class. Check child, youth and other age conditions for each traveller rather than multiplying one adult price. Residence details and product eligibility still matter, so use the current purchase conditions rather than assuming the new name makes every pass valid everywhere.

A pass and a reservation are different

A pass provides travel entitlement under its conditions. Some trains also require a reservation or supplement, and availability can be limited. Check your important trains before buying. Some Plus Pass products include reservation credits subject to their own terms; this is not unlimited access to any seat.

Compare the total cost

Add the pass and necessary reservations, then compare individual tickets for the same dates and travellers. Consider the flexibility you actually need. A short fixed itinerary can favour separate tickets, while a multi-stop journey may justify a pass; neither result is guaranteed.

Prepare a mobile pass correctly

Follow the official Rail Planner activation and journey-adding steps before boarding. Keep required reservations separately accessible and check current connectivity requirements. An itinerary saved in a planner is not a booked seat or proof that a train still has availability.

Before paying or changing a booking

Check passenger details, residency information, validity, refund and exchange terms, and any reservation cancellation conditions. If you want to upgrade from second to first class, ask about the specific pass and train; paying a difference is not automatically an available option. Use the official transition FAQ for an existing Eurail order. Do not buy a second pass merely because an old article or seller still uses the previous brand name.

Official sources
