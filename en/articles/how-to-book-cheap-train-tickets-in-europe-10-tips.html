Ten practical ways to compare cheaper European train tickets

Saving money works best when the ticket still fits your journey. Use these checks together; none guarantees a discount or makes a restrictive fare flexible.

1. Search the complete itinerary

Enter your actual stations and onward connections. A low fare to the wrong city station can be poor value once the extra transfer is included.

2. Compare nearby departures

If your plans permit it, look at several times and dates. Availability varies, but there is no universal cheapest weekday or booking hour.

3. Check whether booking has opened

Timetable changes and individual operators affect the sales window. A missing fare can mean sales have not started, not that the train is full.

4. Read the fare conditions

Compare train-specific and flexible fares before paying. A non-refundable ticket may be unsuitable when flights, meetings or accommodation are not settled.

5. Add reservations and extras

Include required seats, sleepers, bicycles and local transfers in the total. Check whether an item is already included before buying it twice.

6. Use only valid discounts

Enter the real ages and eligible discount cards for all travellers. An attractive search result is unusable if you cannot show the required proof.

7. Compare seller totals

Compare the same train and fare on the railway and chosen agency. Include booking fees, currency and the service channel you would use for changes.

8. Test a pass against real tickets

Price the journeys you actually expect to take and add reservation costs to the pass. Do not buy extra travel days just because the headline daily figure looks low.

9. Protect connections

Check whether separate tickets change your protection if a train is late. A tiny saving may not justify an unrealistic transfer or a missed last departure.

10. Verify before payment

Review names, stations, dates, class and refund rules, then save the confirmation. Use current official conditions rather than screenshots of an old promotion.
