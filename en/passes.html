Rail passes: compare the options

Compare a multi-country pass, a single-country product and individual tickets for your own itinerary. The cheapest choice depends on available fares, reservations and how often you will travel.

Editorial guidance reviewed against the official sources below on 30 October 2026. Prices and available departures must be checked for your travel date.

Eligibility and pass network

On the official pass website, set your actual country of residence and check the products you can buy. Global and One Country products serve different networks. Do not rely on old Eurail Select descriptions or assume that a country pass covers every operator, metro or cross-border segment. Check the current validity map and residence restrictions.

Official sources: Interrail / Eurail

Swiss Travel Pass and alternatives

SBB offers Swiss Travel Pass and Flex for visitors whose permanent residence is outside Switzerland and Liechtenstein. Coverage includes eligible trains, buses, boats and local transport; panoramic reservations or supplements are separate. Check the validity map for each mountain excursion rather than assuming every summit is free. Compare with individual tickets and Swiss Half Fare Card.

Official sources: SBB

Is the Swiss Travel Pass right for you?

Consider it if you will make frequent covered journeys between several bases and value flexibility. Compare the full itinerary cost first; a pass is not automatically cheaper.

Do not buy it just for one scenic train or a mostly stationary stay. Compare individual tickets and the Swiss Half Fare Card, which requires buying eligible reduced-fare tickets separately.

Choose 3, 4 or 8 days by your travel dates

3 days: a compact trip with travel on three consecutive days. 4 days: travel spread over four consecutive days. 8 days: a longer circuit needing eight consecutive days of coverage. Count calendar travel days, not hotel nights; a longer pass is not automatically better value. SBB also offers 6- and 15-day options. Flex uses selected days within one month, not continuous coverage.

Before choosing a country pass

For a trip mainly in Germany, France, Italy or Switzerland, compare the current country-specific offers on the official sites. Confirm included operators, international extensions, age rules and dates. Do not buy from an old price table: products and names can change, and regional day tickets may have different time or train restrictions.

Official sources: Interrail / Eurail · SBB

A practical break-even calculation

Make two totals for the same itinerary: available individual fares plus extras; and the pass price plus all reservations, supplements and uncovered travel. Include museum or local transport value only if you will actually use the covered benefit. A pass can suit a changing itinerary, but the number of countries alone does not prove it saves money.

Individual fares + extras ↔ pass + reservations + uncovered journeys

Reservations and supplements

Check every train for a compulsory reservation and any supplement before buying the pass. A pass does not itself guarantee an available seat. Standard pass products may require extra payment, while a specifically described bundle may include some reservation costs. Check the exact product, train, class, quota and refund rules; do not apply one fee to all services.

Official sources: Interrail · Interrail / Eurail

Travel days and activation

Count travel days separately from nights at your destination. Compare flexible-day and consecutive-day products, the overall validity period and activation deadline. With a mobile pass, follow the official instructions to activate it and add the journey before boarding; an itinerary saved in a planner alone is not proof of a valid ticket.

Official sources: Interrail
