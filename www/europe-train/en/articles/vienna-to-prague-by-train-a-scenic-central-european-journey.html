Vienna to Prague by Train: A Scenic Central European Journey

The train journey from Vienna to Prague is one of Europe's most underrated rail routes. Covering approximately 250 kilometers through the heart of Central Europe, this 4-hour journey offers stunning landscapes, historic towns, and a glimpse into the region's rich cultural heritage.

Route Overview

  • Distance: ~250 km (155 miles)
  • Duration: 3h 50m - 4h 30m
  • Frequency: Every 2 hours throughout the day
  • Operators: ÖBB (Austrian Federal Railways) and České dráhy (Czech Railways)
  • Price Range: €19 - €75 depending on booking time and class

Train Options

ÖBB Railjet

The premium option for this route, offering:

  • Modern, comfortable carriages with spacious seating
  • Free WiFi and power outlets at every seat
  • On-board dining car serving Austrian and Czech cuisine
  • First class includes complimentary refreshments and newspapers

České dráhy EuroCity

A reliable alternative with:

  • Comfortable seating and ample luggage space
  • Scenic route through the Moravian countryside
  • More affordable pricing compared to Railjet
  • Connections to smaller towns along the route

Scenic Highlights

This journey takes you through some of Central Europe's most beautiful landscapes:

  • Vienna Woods (Wienerwald): Lush forests and rolling hills as you depart Vienna
  • Moravian Vineyards: Endless rows of grapevines covering the hillsides
  • Historic Towns: Glimpses of Břeclav and Brno from the train window
  • Bohemian Countryside: Picturesque villages and farmland approaching Prague

Booking Tips

1. Book Early for Best Prices

Tickets are released 90 days in advance. Early bookings can save up to 60% with Sparschiene (Austria) or First Minute (Czech) fares.

2. Consider a Eurail Pass

If you're visiting multiple countries, a Eurail Global Pass covers both Austria and Czech Republic, making this journey effectively free (just reserve a seat for €3-€10).

3. Check Both Operators

Compare prices on oebb.at and cd.cz. Sometimes one operator offers significantly better deals than the other for the same route.

4. Choose Your Seat Wisely

For the best views, sit on the right side when traveling Vienna → Prague, and the left side when traveling Prague → Vienna.

Station Information

Vienna Hauptbahnhof (Main Station)

  • Modern, spacious station with excellent facilities
  • Direct connections to Vienna U-Bahn (subway)
  • Plenty of shops, cafes, and waiting areas
  • Arrive 20 minutes before departure

Prague hlavní nádraží (Main Station)

  • Historic Art Nouveau building with modern extensions
  • Metro Line C directly connects to the city center
  • Walking distance to Wenceslas Square (15 minutes)
  • Free WiFi and luggage storage available

What to Expect On Board

  • Comfort: Both operators offer comfortable seating with good legroom
  • WiFi: Generally reliable but can be spotty in rural areas
  • Food: Dining car or trolley service available; bring snacks as backup
  • Luggage: Overhead racks and designated luggage areas
  • Border Crossing: No passport checks (both are Schengen countries)

Extending Your Journey

This route connects perfectly with other Central European destinations:

  • Vienna → Budapest: 2h 30m by Railjet (popular extension)
  • Prague → Berlin: 4h 15m by EuroCity
  • Prague → Dresden: 2h 15m (beautiful Elbe Valley route)
  • Vienna → Salzburg: 2h 15m (gateway to the Alps)

Money-Saving Tips

  • Book Sparschiene/First Minute tickets 2-3 months ahead
  • Travel Tuesday-Thursday for lower fares
  • Consider regional trains for a slower but cheaper journey
  • Eurail Pass holders: just pay seat reservation fee
  • Students and seniors: check for discount fares (up to 50% off)

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need a passport for this journey?

No, both Austria and Czech Republic are in the Schengen Area. No border checks, but carry ID as a precaution.

Can I bring a bicycle?

Yes, but reservations are required (€5-€10). Space is limited, so book early.

Is there a night train?

No direct night train on this route. The last evening train departs around 20:00.

What's the best time to travel?

Spring (April-May) and autumn (September-October) offer the best scenery and fewer crowds.

Can I break the journey?

Standard tickets don't allow stopovers, but Eurail Pass holders can hop on and off freely.
